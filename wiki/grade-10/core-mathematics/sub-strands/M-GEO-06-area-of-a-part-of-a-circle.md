# M-GEO-06: Area of a Part of a Circle

**Inquiry question:** How do we use the concept of the area of a part of a circle in real life?

## Curriculum alignment

The design covers the area of an annulus, sector, annular sector, segment, and common region
between two intersecting circles, then applies these areas to real situations. The source
design indicates 12 lessons for this sub-strand.

**Concepts:** radius, diameter, circumference, annulus, sector, central angle, annular sector,
segment, chord, major/minor region, overlap.

**CBC emphasis:** creativity and imagination, learning to learn, communication and
collaboration; responsibility and unity; environmental education, parental engagement, and
peer education.

## Teacher pack

### Learning sequence

1. Build from circle area `pi r^2` and identify the fraction represented by a central angle.
   For a sector, `A = theta/360 x pi r^2`.
2. Subtract circle areas for an annulus; subtract sector areas for an annular sector.
3. Draw a segment and decompose it into a sector minus a triangle. Make clear whether the
   required region is minor or major.
4. For two intersecting circles, label the common region and split it into familiar pieces
   before calculating. Use diagrams and units at every step.

### Low-resource activity

Cut concentric paper circles and colour annular sectors. A learner-made dartboard can provide
areas and scoring regions; groups explain why changing radius changes area quadratically.

### Kenyan context

Model the sweep of a car wiper as an annular sector, or a circular planting bed with a central
path as an annulus. Use local materials and discuss safe, sustainable reuse.

### Check for understanding

- Decide whether a diagram asks for a sector, segment, annulus, or annular sector.
- Convert the angle fraction before substituting in `pi r^2`.
- Subtract the correct inner region and report square units.
- Explain how two overlapping circles can be decomposed without double-counting.

### Common misconceptions

- Using arc length when area is requested.
- Forgetting to square radius or using diameter as radius.
- Using `theta/180` instead of `theta/360` for a sector.
- Subtracting the wrong triangle for a segment or counting an overlap twice.

## Worked example

Find the area of a 90 degree sector of radius 4 cm:
`A = 90/360 x pi x 4^2 = 4pi cm^2`, approximately `12.57 cm^2`. Keep `pi` until the final
rounding unless the task specifies a decimal.

## SMS teaching pack

**SMS 1/3:** `M-GEO-06` Sector area=(theta/360)*pi*r^2. Annulus area=pi(R^2-r^2). Use radius, not diameter.

**SMS 2/3:** An annular sector is outer sector minus inner sector. A segment is sector minus triangle; sketch before subtracting.

**SMS 3/3:** Try radius 6 cm, angle 60°. Find sector area in terms of pi. Give square units.
