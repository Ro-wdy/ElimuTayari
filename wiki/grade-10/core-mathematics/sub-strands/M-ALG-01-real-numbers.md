# M-ALG-01: Real Numbers

**Inquiry question:** How do we use real numbers in day-to-day activities?

## Curriculum alignment

The design focuses on classifying whole numbers as odd, even, prime, or composite;
classifying real numbers as rational or irrational; finding reciprocals by division, tables,
and calculators; applying reciprocals in computations; and promoting the use of real numbers
in daily activities.

**Concepts:** whole numbers, odd and even numbers, prime and composite numbers, rational and
irrational numbers, real-number line, reciprocal, zero denominator.

**CBC emphasis:** communication and collaboration, critical thinking and problem solving,
digital literacy; unity, responsibility, and self-esteem through participation and careful
use of tables/calculators.

## Teacher pack

### Learning sequence

1. Sort locally familiar counts (learners present, books, goals scored) into whole numbers;
   test odd/even, then identify prime and composite cases.
2. Place fractions, terminating decimals, recurring decimals, and non-terminating
   non-recurring decimals on a number line. Use the test "can it be written as p/q, q != 0?"
   to distinguish rational from irrational.
3. Define a reciprocal as the multiplicative inverse. Find reciprocals by division, then
   compare a mathematical table with a calculator and estimate whether the answer is sensible.
4. Apply reciprocals to simplify division and solve short measurement or rate problems.

### Low-resource activity

Write numbers on bottle tops or paper cards. In groups, learners make three hoops labelled
`whole-number property`, `rational/irrational`, and `reciprocal`. A card can belong to more
than one hoop. Each group must explain one placement and one possible misconception.

### Kenyan context

Use a shop receipt to distinguish exact counts (whole numbers), measured quantities that may
be written as fractions/decimals (rational numbers), and approximations such as a diagonal
length involving sqrt(2) (irrational). Emphasise that rounding is a representation choice,
not a change to the underlying number.

### Check for understanding

- Classify 0, 1, 2, 9, -3, 3/4, 0.125, 0.333..., and sqrt(2), and explain each choice.
- Ask: why is 1 neither prime nor composite? Why does 0 have no reciprocal?
- Give 5/8 and -2 and request both reciprocal and a multiplication check.
- Exit ticket: one exact real-life quantity and one approximate quantity, with a reason.

### Common misconceptions

- Treating 1 as prime; prime numbers have exactly two positive factors.
- Calling every decimal irrational; terminating and recurring decimals are rational.
- Writing the reciprocal of a number as its negative; reciprocal and additive inverse are
  different ideas.
- Dividing by zero or claiming that zero's reciprocal is zero.

## Worked example

Classify `0.272727...` and find its reciprocal.

The decimal repeats, so it is rational (`0.272727... = 3/11`). Its reciprocal is `11/3`,
because `(3/11) x (11/3) = 1`. A calculator may display `3.666...`; the fraction is the
exact form.

## SMS teaching pack

**SMS 1/3:** `M-ALG-01` Classify numbers: 1 is neither prime nor composite. Terminating or recurring decimals are rational; sqrt(2) is irrational.

**SMS 2/3:** A reciprocal multiplies a non-zero number to give 1. Reciprocal of a/b is b/a. Zero has no reciprocal. Check by multiplication.

**SMS 3/3:** Try: classify 0, 7, 9, 3/4 and sqrt(2). Find reciprocal of 5/8. Explain one answer to a partner.
