# M-ALG-03: Quadratic Expressions and Equations

**Inquiry question:** How do we use equations in daily life?

## Curriculum alignment

The design covers forming quadratic expressions, deriving and applying quadratic identities
from area, factorising quadratic expressions, forming quadratic equations, solving them by
factorisation, and applying/exploring quadratic equations in real-life situations.

**Concepts:** quadratic term, coefficient, constant, identity, factor, zero-product property,
roots/solutions, modelling assumptions.

**CBC emphasis:** critical thinking and problem solving, communication and collaboration;
unity and respect through shared reasoning; social cohesion through real-life cases.

## Knowledge graph

- **Prerequisites:** [Real Numbers](./M-ALG-01-real-numbers.md) and

## Teacher pack

### Learning sequence

1. Translate statements and rectangular areas into expressions of degree two. Identify the
   coefficient of `x^2`, the linear term, and the constant.
2. Derive identities by expanding areas: `(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2`,
   `(a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2`, and `a^2-b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)`.
3. Factorise by common factor, grouping, difference of squares, and the general pattern
   `x^2 + (p+q)x + pq = (x+p)(x+q)`.
4. Form equations from roots, statements, and simple contexts. Set the equation to zero,
   factorise, apply the zero-product property, and reject solutions that violate the context.

### Low-resource activity

Cut a square and two rectangles from paper. Rearrange them to show the area identity for
`(x+3)^2`. Learners label each piece, write the expanded form, then reverse the process to
factorise it.

### Kenyan context

Model the dimensions of a rectangular school garden when one side is increased by a fixed
amount. A budget or fencing problem can produce a quadratic equation; ask learners to state
which root is physically meaningful and why.

### Check for understanding

- Expand and factorise `(x+4)^2` in both directions.
- Factorise `x^2 - 5x + 6` and solve the associated equation.
- Turn a short garden or area statement into an equation before solving it.
- Require a substitution check for every proposed root.

### Common misconceptions

- Calling an expression an equation without an equals sign.
- Losing the middle term when expanding a square.
- Applying the zero-product property before putting the equation in product-equals-zero form.
- Reporting a negative length or an extraneous root without checking the context.

## Worked example

Solve `x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0`.

Find two numbers whose product is `6` and sum is `-5`: `-2` and `-3`. Thus
`(x-2)(x-3)=0`, so `x=2` or `x=3`. Substitute both values into the original equation;
both satisfy it. If `x` represents a length, retain only values allowed by the stated model.

## SMS teaching pack

**SMS 1/3:** `M-ALG-03` A quadratic has an x^2 term. Key identities: (a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2; a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b).

**SMS 2/3:** To solve by factorisation, write product=0, factorise, then set each factor to zero. Check roots in the original equation.

**SMS 3/3:** Try: factorise x^2-7x+12 and solve it. Explain which roots are possible if x is a length.
