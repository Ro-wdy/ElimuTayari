# M-GEO-08: Vectors I

**Inquiry questions:** What are vectors? Where are vectors used in real-life situations?

## Curriculum alignment

The design covers vector and scalar quantities, notation and geometric representation,
equivalent vectors, addition, multiplication by a scalar, column and position vectors,
magnitude, midpoint, translation vectors, and real-life use.

**Concepts:** magnitude, direction, scalar, directed segment, equivalent vectors, resultant,
column vector, position vector, midpoint, translation.

**CBC emphasis:** learning to learn and self-efficacy; responsibility and unity; environmental
education and road safety.

## Knowledge graph

- **Related:** [Rotation](./M-GEO-03-rotation.md), where directed movement is visualised.
- **Leads to:** [Linear Motion](./M-GEO-09-linear-motion.md).

## Teacher pack

### Learning sequence

1. Sort quantities with direction (displacement, velocity) from quantities without direction
   (distance, speed, mass). Use arrows to show magnitude and direction.
2. Write vectors in column form and draw them on grids. Equivalent vectors have equal length and
   direction even when placed in different locations.
3. Add vectors head-to-tail or component-wise; multiply by a scalar and interpret a negative
   scalar as reversal of direction.
4. Use position vectors to find translations, midpoint, and magnitude
   `|a|=sqrt(x^2+y^2)`. Connect road arrows and movement diagrams to safety.

### Low-resource activity

Draw a grid on cardboard. Learners place arrow strips for two journeys, join them head-to-tail,
and compare the resultant with a direct arrow. Repeat with a reversed or doubled arrow.

### Kenyan context

Map a walking route around the school or describe a road sign's direction. Distinguish total
distance walked from displacement from start to finish.

### Check for understanding

- Decide whether a quantity is scalar or vector and justify it.
- Add two column vectors and draw the resultant.
- Find a translation vector from a point and its image.
- Compute magnitude and midpoint, then verify on a grid.

### Common misconceptions

- Treating a vector as a number with no direction.
- Adding magnitudes while ignoring direction/components.
- Confusing a position vector with the point's coordinate label.
- Thinking a negative scalar means a negative length rather than reversed direction.

## Worked example

For `a=(3,4)` as a column vector, `|a|=sqrt(3^2+4^2)=5`. For `b=(-3,-4)`, the magnitude is
also 5 but the direction is opposite. Equal magnitudes alone do not make vectors equivalent.

## SMS teaching pack

**SMS 1/3:** `M-GEO-08` A scalar has size only; a vector has size and direction. Column vector (x,y) shows horizontal/vertical change.

**SMS 2/3:** Add vectors by components or head-to-tail. Magnitude of (x,y) is sqrt(x^2+y^2). A negative scalar reverses direction.

**SMS 3/3:** Try a=(6,8). Find |a|. Is b=(-6,-8) equivalent to a? Explain using magnitude and direction.
