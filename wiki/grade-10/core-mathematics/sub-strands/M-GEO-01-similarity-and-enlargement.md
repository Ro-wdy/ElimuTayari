# M-GEO-01: Similarity and Enlargement

**Inquiry question:** How are similarity and enlargement applied in day-to-day life?

## Curriculum alignment

Learners determine the centre of enlargement and linear scale factor for similar figures,
construct an image from a centre and scale factor, determine area and volume scale factors,
relate the three scale factors, and apply similarity and enlargement to real situations.

**Concepts:** corresponding sides and angles, similar figures, centre of enlargement, linear
scale factor (LSF), area scale factor (ASF), volume scale factor (VSF), reduction.

**CBC emphasis:** communication and collaboration, creativity and imagination; responsibility
and integrity in measurements; social cohesion and environmental education.

## Teacher pack

### Learning sequence

1. Compare pairs of figures: equal corresponding angles and a constant ratio of corresponding
   lengths make figures similar.
2. Join corresponding points of an object and image to locate the centre of enlargement.
   Measure a pair of corresponding lengths to find `k = image/original`.
3. Construct an image by drawing rays from the centre and marking each distance `k` times the
   original. On a Cartesian plane, use `P' = O + k(P-O)`.
4. Connect scale factors: lengths use `k`, areas use `k^2`, and volumes use `k^3`. Interpret
   whether a model is enlarged or reduced and check units.

### Low-resource activity

Use graph paper and a centre point to enlarge a triangle for `k = 2` and reduce it for
`k = 1/2`. Learners measure one side, the area, and (with a paper solid) volume to discover
the `k`, `k^2`, `k^3` pattern.

### Kenyan context

Use a local map, a scaled school plan, or a model classroom. Ask learners to separate a
linear map scale from an area estimate and explain why the numerical factor is squared for
land area.

### Check for understanding

- Find `k` from two corresponding side lengths and classify enlargement/reduction.
- Construct the image of a point or triangle for a stated centre and `k`.
- Given `k`, predict area and volume factors before calculating.
- Explain why a map distance and a map area cannot use the same scale factor.

### Common misconceptions

- Using `k` for area or volume instead of `k^2` or `k^3`.
- Measuring from the page edge instead of the centre of enlargement.
- Assuming similar figures must face the same direction.
- Mixing units before applying a scale factor.

## Worked example

A model is made with linear scale factor `k = 3/5`. If the original solid has volume `125 cm^3`,
the model volume is `125 x (3/5)^3 = 125 x 27/125 = 27 cm^3`. The linear reduction is not a
27/125 reduction; that factor belongs to volume.

## SMS teaching pack

**SMS 1/3:** `M-GEO-01` Similar figures have equal corresponding angles and proportional sides. LSF k=image length/original length.

**SMS 2/3:** If lengths scale by k, areas scale by k^2 and volumes by k^3. Use the centre of enlargement to draw rays.

**SMS 3/3:** Try k=2/3 for a solid of volume 216 cm3. Find model volume. Explain why the answer uses k^3.
