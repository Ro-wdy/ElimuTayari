# M-GEO-09: Linear Motion

**Inquiry questions:** What is speed? How do we use displacement-time graphs in real life?
How do we use velocity-time graphs?

## Curriculum alignment

Learners explain distance, displacement, speed, velocity, and acceleration in real situations;
determine velocity and acceleration; draw and interpret displacement-time and velocity-time
graphs; determine relative speed of two moving bodies; and appreciate linear motion in daily life.

**Concepts:** distance, displacement, speed, velocity, acceleration, gradient, area under a
velocity-time graph, displacement-time graph, velocity-time graph, relative speed.

**CBC emphasis:** communication and collaboration, self-efficacy, critical thinking and
problem solving; responsibility, integrity, and unity; sport, safety/security, and road safety.

## Teacher pack

### Learning sequence

1. Move an object along two routes. Compare distance (path length) with displacement (directed
   change from start). Define speed as distance/time and velocity as displacement/time.
2. Define acceleration as change in velocity/time. Record a short race or walking experiment,
   keeping units and timing method visible.
3. Plot displacement against time. Gradient gives velocity; a horizontal section means no
   displacement change. Plot velocity against time; gradient gives acceleration and area gives
   displacement when the graph interpretation allows it.
4. Compare two moving bodies and distinguish relative speed when they move in the same versus
   opposite directions. Connect careful overtaking to road safety.

### Low-resource activity

Use a phone stopwatch (or a wall clock) and a measured corridor. Teams record time at fixed
positions, create a displacement-time graph, and annotate where motion is faster or stopped.

### Kenyan context

Use a school race, matatu journey, or safe road-observation scenario. Do not ask learners to
stand in traffic; use a controlled school track or supplied data for overtaking examples.

### Check for understanding

- Label a quantity as distance/displacement or speed/velocity and state its units.
- Calculate velocity and acceleration from a table of measurements.
- Read gradient and key intervals on each graph.
- Add/subtract speeds correctly for same/opposite directions and explain the safety implication.

### Common misconceptions

- Treating distance and displacement as interchangeable.
- Calling speed a vector or velocity a scalar.
- Reading the height of a displacement-time graph as speed; speed is its gradient.
- Mixing km/h and m/s or using relative-speed rules without checking direction.

## Worked example

A learner travels 60 m east in 12 s. Average speed is `60/12=5 m/s`; if the trip is straight
east, average velocity is `5 m/s east`. If the learner returns to the start, total distance
increases but final displacement becomes zero.

## SMS teaching pack

**SMS 1/3:** `M-GEO-09` Speed=distance/time; velocity=displacement/time; acceleration=change in velocity/time. Include units.

**SMS 2/3:** On displacement-time graph, gradient=velocity. On velocity-time graph, gradient=acceleration; area can give displacement.

**SMS 3/3:** Try 60 m east in 12 s: find speed and velocity. If the learner returns to start, what is final displacement?
