# M-GEO-02: Reflection and Congruence

**Inquiry questions:** How do we use reflection in day-to-day life? Where do we use
congruence in real life?

## Curriculum alignment

The design covers lines and planes of symmetry, properties of reflection, drawing images from
an object and mirror line on paper and the Cartesian plane, finding the equation of a mirror
line, and triangle congruence tests.

**Concepts:** line/plane symmetry, mirror line, perpendicular distance, invariant points,
Cartesian reflection, congruence, SSS, SAS, AAS/ASA, RHS.

**CBC emphasis:** learning to learn and creativity; responsibility and respect; road safety,
environment, and technology.

## Knowledge graph

- **Prerequisite:** [Similarity and Enlargement](./M-GEO-01-similarity-and-enlargement.md).
- **Related:** [Rotation](./M-GEO-03-rotation.md), another congruent transformation.

## Teacher pack

### Learning sequence

1. Fold or mirror plane figures to identify lines of symmetry and discuss the meaning of
   equal perpendicular distances from a mirror line.
2. Reflect points and shapes on a grid. Make the rule visible: a point and its image lie on
   opposite sides of the mirror line at equal perpendicular distance.
3. For a horizontal or vertical mirror, use coordinate rules (for example, across the
   x-axis `(x,y) -> (x,-y)`). For a slanted mirror, construct the perpendicular bisector of
   a point and its image, then derive the line equation.
4. Compare identical cut-outs and establish when triangles are congruent using SSS, SAS,
   AAS/ASA, and RHS. Congruence preserves lengths and angles, not orientation.

### Low-resource activity

Place tracing paper over a grid drawing, fold on a marked mirror line, and trace the image.
Learners open the fold, measure matching distances, and label which points are invariant.

### Kenyan context

Discuss plane mirrors and side mirrors used by drivers. Connect a safe mirror position to
reflection while making clear that a road-safety decision should follow traffic guidance,
not a classroom diagram alone.

### Check for understanding

- Reflect a triangle in `y = 0` and list all image coordinates.
- Identify lines of symmetry for a rectangle, square, kite, and regular polygon.
- Decide which data set proves triangle congruence and name the test.
- Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of a point-image pair.

### Common misconceptions

- Sliding a shape along the mirror line instead of preserving perpendicular distance.
- Treating a reflection as a rotation or translation.
- Calling two figures congruent because their areas match.
- Using SSA as a general triangle congruence test; it is not sufficient.

## Worked example

Reflect `A(2, 1)` across the x-axis. The x-coordinate stays 2 and the signed y-coordinate
changes sign, so `A'(2, -1)`. The midpoint `(2,0)` lies on the mirror line and segment `AA'`
is perpendicular to it. Use those two checks to catch a plotting error.

## SMS teaching pack

**SMS 1/3:** `M-GEO-02` Reflection keeps perpendicular distance to the mirror line equal. Points on the mirror line stay fixed.

**SMS 2/3:** Across x-axis: (x,y)->(x,-y). Triangle tests: SSS, SAS, AAS/ASA and RHS. Congruent means same size and shape.

**SMS 3/3:** Try reflect B(-3,4) in y=0. Name one line of symmetry of a square and one valid triangle congruence test.
