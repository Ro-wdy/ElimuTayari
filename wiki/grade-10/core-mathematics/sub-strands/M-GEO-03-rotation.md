# M-GEO-03: Rotation

**Inquiry question:** How is rotation applied in real-life situations?

## Curriculum alignment

Learners determine properties of rotation, rotate an object about a given centre and angle
on paper and the Cartesian plane, find the centre and angle from an object-image pair,
determine rotational symmetry of plane figures and solids, deduce congruence from rotation,
and appreciate real-life applications.

**Concepts:** centre, angle, direction (clockwise/anticlockwise), invariant distance and
angle, order of rotational symmetry, axis of symmetry, congruence.

**CBC emphasis:** critical thinking and problem solving, communication and collaboration;
responsibility and respect; environmental education.

## Knowledge graph

- **Prerequisite:** [Reflection and Congruence](./M-GEO-02-reflection-and-congruence.md).

## Teacher pack

### Learning sequence

1. Use an improvised clock to name centre, angle, and direction. Check that rotation keeps
   distances from the centre and angle relationships unchanged.
2. Rotate a paper shape about a marked centre, then reproduce it on a Cartesian grid. Record
   the mapping rather than relying on a visual guess.
3. Given an object and image, join corresponding points and construct perpendicular bisectors
   to locate the centre; measure the angle and state direction.
4. Turn plane figures and regular solids through a full turn to count positions that match.
   Use that count as the order of rotational symmetry. A rotation is a congruent transformation.

### Low-resource activity

Pin a paper triangle to a board with a drawing pin, mark a ray, and rotate it by quarter-turns.
Learners trace each position, count the matching positions in one full turn, and compare with
a clock face or wheel.

### Kenyan context

Connect rotation to bicycle wheels, water taps, gears, and the hands of a clock. Ask which
parts must remain fixed for the object to rotate safely.

### Check for understanding

- Rotate a point about the origin through 90 degrees anticlockwise and plot the result.
- Determine the order of rotational symmetry of a rectangle, square, and equilateral triangle.
- Given two shapes, construct or estimate the centre and angle of rotation.
- Explain why a rotation preserves congruence.

### Common misconceptions

- Measuring the angle from the wrong centre or reversing direction.
- Counting the starting position incorrectly when finding order.
- Confusing rotational symmetry with line symmetry.
- Moving the centre during a multi-point rotation.

## Worked example

Rotate `P(3,1)` 90 degrees anticlockwise about the origin. The coordinate rule is
`(x,y) -> (-y,x)`, so `P'(-1,3)`. Check that both points are the same distance from the
origin and that the turn is anticlockwise.
