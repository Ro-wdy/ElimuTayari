# M-GEO-04: Trigonometry 1

**Inquiry questions:** What is trigonometry? How do we use trigonometry in real-life
situations?

## Curriculum alignment

The design expects learners to determine sine, cosine, and tangent for acute angles using
tables and calculators; relate sines and cosines of complementary angles; relate the three
ratios; derive special-angle ratios for 30, 45, 60, and 90 degrees using triangles; and solve
triangle, elevation, and depression situations.

**Concepts:** opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse, sine, cosine, tangent, complementary angles,
special angles, angle of elevation, angle of depression, clinometer.

**CBC emphasis:** creativity and imagination, critical thinking and problem solving, digital
literacy; responsibility and unity; environmental education and parental engagement.

## Teacher pack

### Learning sequence

1. Identify right-angled triangles in the environment. Name the side opposite and adjacent
   to a chosen acute angle and the hypotenuse.
2. Build `sin(theta)=opposite/hypotenuse`, `cos(theta)=adjacent/hypotenuse`, and
   `tan(theta)=opposite/adjacent`. Use a diagram before a calculator.
3. Pair each acute angle with its complement and observe `sin(theta)=cos(90-theta)`;
   connect `tan(theta)=sin(theta)/cos(theta)` where defined.
4. Derive special angles from an isosceles right triangle and an equilateral triangle, then
   apply ratios to triangle, elevation, and depression problems.

### Low-resource activity

Make a simple clinometer from a protractor, string, and a small weight. Measure the angle to
the top of a school object, record the horizontal distance, and discuss measurement limits
before estimating height.

### Kenyan context

Estimate the height of a tree, flagpole, or classroom using a clinometer. Discuss safe places
to stand, units, and why a measured angle should be recorded before a calculation.

### Check for understanding

- Label a triangle for a stated angle and select the correct ratio.
- Use a table/calculator to find a ratio and its complementary relationship.
- Reconstruct 30, 45, and 60 degree ratios from reference triangles.
- Draw the line of sight for elevation/depression and identify the right triangle.

### Common misconceptions

- Choosing opposite/adjacent relative to the wrong angle.
- Using a calculator in radian mode for degree questions.
- Treating angle of depression as a negative angle in the triangle.
- Rounding an intermediate ratio too early.

## Worked example

In a right triangle, the side opposite angle `theta` is 6 cm and the hypotenuse is 10 cm.
Then `sin(theta)=6/10=0.6`, so `theta` is approximately 36.9 degrees in degree mode. Check
that `theta` is acute and that the remaining side is consistent with Pythagoras.
