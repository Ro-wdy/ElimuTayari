# M-GEO-05: Area of Polygons

**Inquiry questions:** How do we work out the area of polygons? How do we apply area of
polygons in real-life situations?

## Curriculum alignment

Learners derive and use `A = 1/2 ab sin C` for a triangle with two sides and an included
angle, use Heron's formula for a triangle from its three sides, find areas of quadrilaterals,
regular heptagons and octagons, find areas of irregular polygons, and apply the ideas in life.

**Concepts:** base and height, included angle, `1/2 ab sin C`, semiperimeter, Heron's formula,
quadrilateral area, regular polygon, apothem, decomposition.

**CBC emphasis:** critical thinking and problem solving, creativity and imagination, learning
to learn; patriotism, responsibility, and peace; environmental education.

## Teacher pack

### Learning sequence

1. Review triangle and quadrilateral area, stressing that a perpendicular height is required.
2. Drop an altitude in a triangle with sides `a`, `b`, included angle `C` to derive
   `A = 1/2 ab sin C`.
3. For three known sides, calculate `s=(a+b+c)/2`, then `A=sqrt(s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c))`.
4. Split quadrilaterals and irregular polygons into triangles or familiar shapes. For regular
   polygons use `A=1/2 x perimeter x apothem`; check whether the apothem is provided or derived.

### Low-resource activity

Trace polygon-shaped objects from the school environment. Groups decompose each outline into
triangles/rectangles, label measured lengths, calculate area, and compare methods.

### Kenyan context

Estimate the area of a school garden, football training plot, or irregular compound boundary.
Ask learners to record measurement uncertainty and explain why an estimate is appropriate.

### Check for understanding

- Choose between base-height, included-angle, Heron, and decomposition methods.
- Calculate a triangle area from two sides and an included angle.
- Calculate and use a semiperimeter without confusing it with perimeter.
- Explain how a regular polygon's apothem changes the area calculation.

### Common misconceptions

- Using the side opposite the angle instead of the two sides enclosing it in `1/2 ab sin C`.
- Substituting perimeter where Heron's formula needs semiperimeter.
- Assuming every polygon is regular.
- Giving square units as linear units.

## Worked example

For a triangle with sides 7 cm and 10 cm enclosing 30 degrees,
`A=1/2 x 7 x 10 x sin(30)=17.5 cm^2`. The angle must be the included angle; if it is not,
the formula has been applied to the wrong data.

## SMS teaching pack

**SMS 1/3:** `M-GEO-05` Triangle with sides a,b and included C: A=1/2 ab sin(C). Use the angle between a and b.

**SMS 2/3:** Heron: s=(a+b+c)/2; A=sqrt[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]. For regular polygons, A=1/2 x perimeter x apothem.

**SMS 3/3:** Try a=8,b=5,C=30°. Find triangle area. State the units and why C must be included between the sides.
